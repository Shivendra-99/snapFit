How to make a PDF smaller without making it unreadable

By Shivendra Kumar Sonkar · Published 2026-11-02

Application portals, scholarship forms and email attachments often cap uploads at 1 or 2 MB, while a scanned certificate easily weighs 8 MB. Before you reach for a compressor it helps to understand why the file is big, because that tells you how much you can safely shrink it.

Why PDFs are large

  • Scanned pages are photographs. A scan or phone photo saved as a PDF is a full-resolution image on every page. These files are large and shrink very well.
  • Embedded images. A document exported from Word or Slides with high-resolution pictures carries them at full size, even if they appear small on the page.
  • Embedded fonts and metadata. These add a modest amount, and rarely matter beyond a few hundred kilobytes.
  • Text-only PDFs are already tiny. A typed ten-page document is often under 100 KB. If yours is large, there are images inside it.

The trade-off you should know about

There are two ways to compress a PDF. The first re-encodes the images inside it and leaves the text as real text. The second renders each page to a picture, compresses that picture, and builds a new PDF from the pictures. The second is simple, works on any file, and gives predictable sizes — but the result is a set of page images, so the text can no longer be selected, searched or copied.

SnapFit's PDF compressor works the second way, entirely in your browser. That is the right choice for scanned documents, which were already images, and for forms that only need to be looked at. It is the wrong choice if you need to keep searchable text, so keep your original file as well and use the compressed copy only for the upload that demands a small size.

Getting the size down in practice

  1. Start from the best original you have. Compressing a compressed file compounds the loss.
  2. Use the quality slider and watch the preview. Lower the quality until the text is only just comfortably legible, then raise it one notch. Small print, stamps and signatures degrade first.
  3. Check the page count. Remove blank or duplicate pages before compressing; every page costs size.
  4. Check the number you need. If the limit is 1 MB and you reach 900 KB, stop. Extra compression only costs legibility.

Tips for documents you will upload

  • Scan in black and white or greyscale for text documents; colour is rarely needed and triples the data.
  • Scan at a moderate resolution. Roughly 150–200 DPI is readable for most forms; 600 DPI is overkill.
  • Crop away the dark scanner borders — they are pure waste.
  • Open the final file once and read it at 100% zoom before uploading. If you can read it, so can the verifier.

Is it safe to compress private documents online?

That depends on whether the file is uploaded. Many compression websites send your PDF to a server. SnapFit does the work on your own device, so identity documents, mark sheets and certificates are not transmitted. You can confirm this yourself: open your browser's developer tools, watch the Network tab while compressing, and you will see no upload of your file.
