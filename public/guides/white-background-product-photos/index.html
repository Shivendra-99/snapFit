White-background product photos: a checklist for online sellers

By Shivendra Kumar Sonkar · Published 2026-11-02

New sellers on Amazon.in, Flipkart, Meesho, Myntra and similar marketplaces quickly learn that the main listing image has rules, and that a better photo sells more than a lower price. The good news: you do not need a studio. You need consistent light, a clean backdrop and a few habits. Marketplaces differ and change their rules, so treat this as a general checklist and confirm the specifics in your seller dashboard's image guidelines.

What marketplaces generally ask for

  • A clean, usually white, background on the main image, with the product the only subject.
  • The product fills most of the frame, centred, without being cut off at the edges.
  • No overlaid text, logos, watermarks, badges or borders on the main image. Promotional graphics belong on secondary images, if allowed at all.
  • Sharp, evenly lit, accurate colour. Returns often come from a colour that did not match the photo.
  • A minimum resolution and a square or near-square shape, so the platform's zoom works. The exact figures vary by marketplace — the per-marketplace pages in SnapFit list the sizes its presets use.

Shooting at home

  1. Use a window, not a bulb. Place the product on a table beside a large window in daytime. Soft, indirect daylight gives even light without harsh shadows. Avoid direct sun, which burns highlights.
  2. Make a sweep. Tape a sheet of white card or chart paper to a wall and let it curve onto the table. A curved backdrop has no horizon line, so the background becomes one smooth tone.
  3. Fill the shadows. Hold a white card opposite the window to bounce light back into the dark side of the product.
  4. Steady the phone. Use a stack of books or a cheap tripod and the timer, so the shot is sharp. Wipe the lens first — a smudged lens is the most common cause of a hazy image.
  5. Shoot several angles. Front, back, side, a close-up of texture or labels, and one showing scale. Buyers cannot touch the item, so photos are the only inspection they get.

Cleaning up the background

Even a good sweep rarely photographs as pure white; it comes out slightly grey or cream. Marketplaces and shoppers prefer a true white that blends into the page. SnapFit's listing tool removes the background with an in-browser model, places the product on white and sizes it to the marketplace preset. Check the cut-out edges at full zoom: hair-thin parts, transparent materials such as glass, and white products on a white background are where automatic removal is weakest.

Tips for easier cut-outs: keep the product a different colour from the backdrop where possible, avoid reflective surfaces that mirror the room, and keep a little space between the product and the backdrop's edges.

Mistakes that get listings flagged or ignored

  • Using another seller's or the manufacturer's photo without permission.
  • Heavy filters or retouching that changes the colour or shape of the product.
  • Props or accessories that are not included in the sale, which buyers read as part of the item.
  • Tiny products lost in a big frame; crop in so the item fills the space.
  • Inconsistent images across a catalogue; a uniform look reads as a trustworthy shop.

Before you upload

  1. Is the product sharp and fully inside the frame?
  2. Is the background clean and consistent, with no leftover edges?
  3. Is the colour true to the real item?
  4. Is the image at the marketplace's size and file limit?
  5. Is there nothing on it that the marketplace forbids?
